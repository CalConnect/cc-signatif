
[[delivery]]
== Delivery and discovery

=== General

This clause specifies how {{trusted artifact,trusted artifacts}} and
certificates are delivered to verifiers, and how delegation chains are
discovered and resolved. Delivery mechanisms include compact encodings for
non-connected devices, machine-readable public projections, and chain
discovery strategies.

Delivery and discovery are distinct from verification: a verifier first
obtains the artifact (delivery), then resolves its chain (discovery), then
applies the verification pipeline (<<verification>>).


[[delivery-qr]]
=== Compact encoding (two-dimensional barcode)

For delivery to devices without network connectivity, a {{trusted artifact}}
or its {{passport}} may be encoded as a two-dimensional barcode (e.g., QR
code). The encoding shall:

* be self-contained, carrying the artifact or passport data plus the minimum
  chain necessary for offline verification;
* include error correction sufficient for the expected scanning environment;
* carry a version identifier for encoding evolution.

The maximum data capacity of the barcode format constrains the artifact size.
For larger artifacts, the barcode may carry a compact reference (e.g., a
transparency log sequence number) that a connected verifier resolves.


[[delivery-passport]]
=== Passport

A {{passport}} is the machine-readable public projection of an {{end
certificate}} or {{trusted artifact}}, optimized for compact storage and rapid
presentation. A passport shall:

* carry the certificate or artifact identifier, key fingerprint, scope summary,
  and validity period;
* be expressible in a deterministic, machine-readable format (e.g., JSON-LD or
  CBOR);
* be verifiable against the same trust anchor bundle and transparency log as
  the underlying certificate or artifact.

A passport is a presentation format, not a separate trust object. The
underlying certificate or artifact remains the authoritative source.


[[delivery-chain-discovery]]
=== Chain discovery mechanisms

Chain discovery mechanisms are specified in <<architecture-discovery>>. This
clause specifies the delivery aspects:

* Embedded chain delivery. The artifact carries the full chain inline.
  No additional network access is required for discovery.

* Transparency-log reference delivery. The artifact carries references
  (transparency-log sequence numbers). The verifier fetches the referenced
  certificates from a transparency log or mirror on first encounter.

* Hybrid delivery. The artifact carries the end certificate and immediate
  chain, with transparency-log references for the remaining chain.


[[delivery-connected]]
=== Connected device delivery

For connected devices, artifacts and certificates may be delivered via
network protocols. The delivery protocol shall:

* authenticate the source (e.g., via TLS or equivalent);
* provide integrity protection for the delivered data;
* support caching of certificates and transparency log entries to minimize
  repeated network access.

NOTE: The specific transport protocol is out of scope of this document (see
<<scope>>). Implementations may use any protocol that satisfies the above
requirements.


[[delivery-challenge-response]]
=== Challenge-response

A verifier may challenge a device signer to produce a fresh {{trusted artifact}}
bound to a nonce, proving that the signing key is live and the artifact was
produced on demand.

A challenge-response exchange shall satisfy the following:

* Nonce entropy. The nonce shall have at least 128 bits of entropy and shall
  be generated using a cryptographically secure random source.

* Freshness window. The verifier shall apply a time freshness window
  (see <<verification-freshness>>) to the challenge-response. A response
  received outside the window shall be rejected.

* Nonce binding. The device shall bind the nonce into the {{canonical
  payload}} of the response artifact, such that the signature covers the nonce.

* Replay rejection. The verifier shall reject any response whose nonce does
  not match the challenge nonce, or whose timestamp falls outside the freshness
  window.

* Single-use nonce. The verifier shall not accept two responses for the same
  challenge nonce.


[[delivery-exchange]]
=== Credential exchange

Delivery to a verifier is only half the movement of a credential: a
credential must also reach its subject, and a verifier must be able to
_ask_ for one that is not yet in hand. A _credential exchange_ is a
two-turn protocol between a holder and a coordinator (an issuer about to
issue, or a verifier requesting presentation) in which the holder asks
and the coordinator responds. A credential exchange shall:

* be initiated by the party that wants the credential — an issuer cannot
  push a credential to a subject that has not asked, and a verifier cannot
  obtain one the holder has not presented;
* authenticate the holder by an _identifier-control challenge_: before
  issuing to, or accepting a presentation from, an identifier, the
  coordinator challenges the holder to sign a fresh nonce with the key
  behind that identifier, proving the credential lands on (or comes from)
  the party that controls it;
* satisfy the nonce requirements of <<delivery-challenge-response>> for
  the identifier-control challenge (entropy, binding, single use).

NOTE: The identifier-control challenge is the same construction as the
device-signer challenge-response of <<delivery-challenge-response>>,
generalized from proving a device key is live to proving a holder controls
an identifier. The two-turn shape (ask, then challenge, then deliver)
applies to issuance (the subject collects its own credential) and to
presentation (the holder answers a verifier's request). Exchange _state_
is a service concern: a conversation has state, and a coordinator that
runs exchanges operates a stateful endpoint, which the deployment
manifest shall declare.
