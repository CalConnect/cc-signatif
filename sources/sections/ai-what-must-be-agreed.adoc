== What must be agreed

This annex records what has to be agreed — by a single arrangement, across
arrangements, and infrastructure-wide — for independent trust infrastructures
to interoperate, and where this document addresses each item. It is adapted
from the harmonisation analysis of the _Verifiable credentials for the quality
infrastructure_ exploration{empty}footnote:[An independent, unofficial
exploration of W3C Verifiable Credentials applied to the quality
infrastructure (metrology, accreditation, legal metrology). Its analysis of
what would have to be agreed informed this annex.], which implemented a
three-branch trust graph (metrology, accreditation, legal metrology) end to
end and documented every interoperability obstacle it met.


=== Per arrangement

Within one scheme, the deploying arrangement agrees:

. *One cryptosuite, and one canonical form.* Addressed by
  <<artifact-canonical-payload>> (canonical payload determinism) and
  <<algorithms>> (the registry names what is recognized).

. *One protocol for asking, not just one format for answering.* Delivery of
  a credential to its subject, and presentation to a verifier, are
  conversations. Addressed by <<delivery-exchange>>.

. *One identifier method, and an agreed meaning for resolving it.* The scheme
  declares its identifier method; cross-identifier meaning is the
  cross-arrangement tier below.

. *How a chain crosses from one arrangement into the other.* Addressed by
  cross-domain co-signatures (<<artifact-cosignatures>>) and multi-path
  verification (<<architecture-discovery>> for path enumeration): the chains compose at the
  artifact, not by cross-certification of the roots.

. *What a status value means, not how it is encoded.* Addressed by
  <<revocation-status-list>>: the profile assigns the consequence; the
  encoding carries the value.

. *What the anchors' identifiers are, and how a verifier learns them.*
  Addressed by the trust anchor bundle (<<architecture-anchors>>) and its
  versioned, out-of-band distribution.

. *What a document authorises, as distinct from what it attests.* A type
  evaluation attests; the legal permission to use the instrument comes from
  the competent authority of each jurisdiction. A scheme profile shall state
  the authorisation semantics of each credential type it defines, so a
  verifier never reads an attestation as a licence.

. *What identifies a type, rather than an instrument.* Addressed by the
  model and serial scope dimensions (<<tab-scope-dimensions>>): the type
  identity is a scope dimension of its own, narrowed independently of the
  individual instrument.


=== Across arrangements

Between arrangements that want to recognise each other:

. *Whose timestamps everybody accepts.* Addressed by the time dimension
  (<<time-dimension>>) and external, irrefutable anchoring: the anchored
  commitment is accepted regardless of whose clock produced it.

. *Which copy governs.* Addressed by the transparency log as the record of
  what was true (<<transparency>>): the log's history, provable by
  consistency proof, governs over any presented copy.

. *How long an identifier goes on meaning what it means.* Identifier
  longevity is an institutional commitment the framework cannot create; the
  manifest declares the operator's retention policy, and long-term
  verification (archival re-signing) is specified in the key-lifecycle
  clause.

. *A log of what was true, not a document saying what is true.* This is the
  transparency log's reason for existing (<<transparency>>), including the
  by-hash index by which a verifier confirms every certificate on a path.

. *Who still serves the documents a verification reads.* Dependency
  documents (evaluation reports, calibration states) must remain fetchable
  for the artifact's lifetime; the hash-binding model
  (<<revocation-hash-binding>>) lets a verifier hold the digest, but the
  serving obligation is an operational commitment declared in the manifest.


=== Infrastructure-wide

For the discipline as a whole:

. *One digital representation of the SI.* Unit authority is outside this
  document's scope; schemes reference a common unit authority so quantity
  values compare.

. *Machine-actionable requirements, in the instrument standard itself.*
  When the Recommendation carries its requirements machine-readably, the
  scope condition language (<<scope-conditions>>) evaluates against them
  directly rather than against a transcribed form.

. *A certificate format with international standing.* The purpose of this
  document: a format any conforming verifier can check offline.

. *Measurand and quantity identifiers.* Shared identifiers for measurands
  and quantities, so a certified capability and a claimed measurement name
  the same thing.

. *How uncertainty travels, dependencies included.* At the calibration
  tier, uncertainty propagation across dependency chains is a metrological
  concern this framework carries (as signed measurement content) but does
  not compute; the state-binding model preserves the dependency structure a
  GUM propagation would need.


=== Reading of the tiers

The pattern across the tiers: the per-arrangement items are profile
decisions this document's requirements already name; the cross-arrangement
items are what the transparency log, the anchoring model, and the co-signature
composition exist to settle; the infrastructure-wide items are institutional
commitments no framework can create, only declare. A scheme that claims
conformance to this document has answered the first tier by construction,
and has the machinery to answer the second; the third tier is the standards
and institutional work that remains genuinely open.
